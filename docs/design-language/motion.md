# Motion language

Motion clarifies change and gives the interface life. It is not ambient decoration.

## Productive motion

Use for frequent actions: hover, press, selection, expansion, table updates, popovers, and drawer movement. Productive motion is short, subtle, and easy to ignore after repeated use.

## Expressive motion

Reserve for occasional moments: onboarding, completion, major progress, first entry, and a significant context change. Expression may combine movement, opacity, shape, and color but must preserve orientation.

## Rules

- Entrances show where content belongs; exits are shorter and less prominent.
- An overlay should appear from its trigger or spatial owner.
- Equivalent interactions use equivalent motion.
- Avoid bounce, perpetual floating, and large parallax in task-heavy areas.
- Reduced-motion preference removes distance and duration while preserving state feedback.
- Small controls move less and finish sooner than large surfaces.
